Table linen is mostly a sizing problem. Get the drop right and almost any combination of cloth, runner and placemats looks deliberate; get it wrong and good fabric looks like a mistake.

This guide covers the arithmetic first, then the layering choices.

## Sizing a tablecloth

**Tablecloth length = table length + (2 x drop)**

**Tablecloth width = table width + (2 x drop)**

The drop is the overhang on each side. Choose it by occasion:

| Setting | Drop | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Everyday / casual | 6-8 in | Clears laps, easy to wash and store |
| Semi-formal | 10-12 in | The most common choice for dinner |
| Formal | 12-15 in | Reaches roughly chair-seat height |
| Event / buffet | To the floor | Usually 29-30 in on a standard-height table |

A drop past 15 inches on a dining table ends up in people's laps. Save floor-length for buffet and display tables where nobody sits.

### Common Indian dining tables

| Table | Seats | Size (in) | Tablecloth at 10 in drop |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Square | 4 | 36 x 36 | 56 x 56 |
| Rectangular small | 4 | 48 x 30 | 68 x 50 |
| Rectangular | 6 | 60 x 36 | 80 x 56 |
| Rectangular large | 8 | 72 x 36 | 92 x 56 |
| Rectangular banquet | 10 | 96 x 40 | 116 x 60 |
| Round | 4 | 42 diameter | 62 diameter |
| Round | 6 | 54 diameter | 74 diameter |
| Round | 8 | 60 diameter | 80 diameter |

For a round table, the cloth is a circle of **table diameter + (2 x drop)**. A square cloth on a round table is a legitimate look, but the four corners will hang notably lower than the sides - plan for it rather than discover it.

## Sizing a runner

A runner sits along the length of the table, over a cloth or straight onto bare wood.

- **Length:** table length + 12 to 24 inches, so 6-12 inches overhang at each end. On a table with a decorative edge, stopping the runner 6 inches short at each end instead also reads well - just be consistent.
- **Width:** roughly **one third of the table width**. On a 36 inch table that is 12-14 inches.
- **Across a round table:** a runner can also run across the diameter, or two runners can cross at the centre.

## Placemats and napkins

| Item | Standard size (in) | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Placemat, rectangular | 12 x 18 | The default. Fits a dinner plate plus cutlery |
| Placemat, round | 15 diameter | Round tables, round plates |
| Cocktail napkin | 6 x 6 | Drinks, canapes |
| Tea / lunch napkin | 16 x 16 | Everyday meals, breakfast |
| Dinner napkin | 20 x 20 | Standard for a full meal |
| Formal napkin | 22 x 22 to 24 x 24 | Multi-course, folded presentations |

Allow **24 inches of table width per person**. A 72 inch table seats three comfortably per side; squeezing four means placemats touch.

## Layering

There are four workable layers. Use two or three, rarely all four.

1. **Silence cloth** (felt or thin quilted underlay). Optional, goes directly on the wood. It deadens the clatter of crockery, protects against heat, and makes the top cloth hang better. Underrated.
2. **Tablecloth.** The main surface.
3. **Runner or placemats.** Use one or the other, not usually both - a runner under placemats reads as clutter unless the placemats are noticeably narrower than the runner.
4. **Napkins.** On the plate, to the left of the forks, or in the glass.

Combinations that work:

- **Everyday:** placemats on bare wood, napkins to the left. Fastest to clear and wash.
- **Family dinner:** tablecloth plus napkins. No runner, no placemats.
- **Semi-formal:** tablecloth, runner down the centre with the serving dishes on it, napkins on the plates.
- **Formal:** silence cloth, tablecloth to a 12-15 inch drop, placemats, napkins folded on the plate.

## A standard place setting

Working outward from the plate, which sits about 1 inch from the table edge:

- **Forks** to the left, in order of use, outermost used first.
- **Knife** immediately right of the plate, blade facing inward toward the plate.
- **Spoon** right of the knife.
- **Dessert spoon and fork** horizontally above the plate.
- **Water glass** above the knife tip; wine glass to its right.
- **Bread plate** above the forks, on the left.
- **Napkin** on the plate, or left of the forks.

For an Indian thali-style setting, the plate or thali sits centred, the spoon goes to the right, and katoris are arranged along the top edge of the thali rather than around it.

## Three napkin folds worth knowing

- **The rectangle.** Fold in half, then half again, lay flat to the left of the forks. Takes two seconds and never looks wrong.
- **The envelope.** Fold into a square, fold the top layer down diagonally, tuck the sides behind. Holds cutlery or a place card neatly.
- **The roll.** Roll on the diagonal and tie with twine, a leaf, or a simple ring. The most forgiving fold on a napkin that has been washed many times and no longer holds a crisp crease.

Cotton and linen hold a fold; synthetic blends do not. If you want structured folds, press the napkins slightly damp beforehand.

## Care notes

- **Wash table linen separately** from towels - lint transfers badly onto flat weaves.
- **Treat food stains before washing**, and never with hot water. See the [bedspread washing guide](/guides/how-to-wash-cotton-bedspreads) for a stain-by-stain table that applies equally here.
- **Iron slightly damp** on medium heat for a crisp finish.
- **Store flat or rolled** rather than tightly folded. Permanent fold lines across a tablecloth centre are hard to remove.
- **Roll runners** around a cardboard tube to avoid a crease down the middle.

## Quick answers

- **What size tablecloth for a 6-seater table?** A 60 x 36 in table needs roughly 80 x 56 in for a 10 inch drop.
- **How much overhang should a tablecloth have?** 6-8 inches for everyday, 10-15 inches for formal.
- **How wide should a table runner be?** About a third of the table width, so 12-14 inches on a standard table.
- **What is the standard dinner napkin size?** 20 x 20 inches; 16 x 16 for everyday meals.
- **Can I use a runner and placemats together?** Yes, if the placemats are clearly narrower than the runner, or the runner crosses the table rather than running along it.
