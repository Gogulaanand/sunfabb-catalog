Cotton bedspreads are forgiving to wash and unforgiving of three specific mistakes: hot water, direct sunlight, and fabric softener. Avoid those and a good bedspread will outlast several mattresses.

This guide assumes an unlined, woven cotton bedspread. Quilted or filled pieces need a gentler cycle and much longer drying, noted at the end.

## The short version

| Step | Setting |
| --- | --- |
| Water temperature | Cold to lukewarm, 30°C maximum |
| Cycle | Gentle or delicate |
| Detergent | Mild liquid, pH-neutral, no optical brighteners |
| Bleach | Never, including oxygen bleach on dyed pieces |
| Fabric softener | Never |
| Spin | Low to medium |
| Drying | Line dry in shade, inside out |
| Ironing | Medium heat while slightly damp |
| Frequency | Every 2-3 weeks in regular use |

## Before the first wash

New cotton is loaded with loose surface dye and finishing starch. The first wash removes both, and it is the wash most likely to go wrong.

1. **Wash it alone.** Not with sheets, not with towels. Loose dye transfers readily on the first cycle.
2. **Use cold water.** Cold water sets dye; warm water lifts it.
3. **Add half a cup of plain salt** to the drum. Salt helps fix reactive dyes on cotton and reduces bleed on subsequent washes.
4. **Skip detergent entirely** on this first cycle if the piece is deeply saturated in colour, such as indigo or madder red. Water alone is enough to shed the excess.
5. **Expect some shrinkage.** Pre-shrunk cotton typically loses 2-3% on the first wash; unsanforized cotton can lose 5-7%. This is why bedspread dimensions are quoted generously.

## The routine wash

**Shake it out first.** Take the bedspread outdoors and shake it hard. Removing dust and hair before it gets wet does more for the final result than any detergent.

**Load it alone or with one light item.** A double bedspread needs room to move; a stuffed drum does not clean, it just creases. If your machine is under 7 kg, wash a queen or king bedspread at a laundromat or by hand instead.

**Choose the right detergent.** A mild liquid detergent dissolves fully in cold water. Powder detergents often do not, and undissolved granules sit against the fabric and bleach out spots. Avoid anything advertising "whitening" or "brightening" on coloured pieces - those are optical brighteners, and they build up as a dull grey cast.

**Use half the dose you think you need.** Excess detergent does not rinse out of a heavy weave. It leaves the cotton stiff and attracts dirt faster.

**Set a low spin.** High spin speeds force deep creases into cotton that then need aggressive ironing to remove.

## Drying, and why shade matters

Direct Indian sun is strong enough to fade dyed cotton visibly within a few hours of exposure. It is also, usefully, a natural bleach and disinfectant. So the rule splits by colour:

- **Dyed or printed bedspreads:** dry in **shade**, turned inside out, on a line or rail with the weight spread across two lines so the fabric does not stretch at the pegs.
- **Undyed, natural or white bedspreads:** direct sun is fine and helps keep whites white.
- **Never tumble dry on high.** If you must use a dryer, use the lowest heat and pull it out while still slightly damp.
- **Do not hang wet cotton over a single narrow rail.** The fold line takes a permanent crease and the weight distorts the weave.

Drying a full-weight cotton bedspread takes 6-10 hours in shade with airflow. During heavy monsoon, run a fan indoors rather than leaving it damp for a second day - cotton left wet for over 24 hours develops mildew that does not fully wash out.

## Ironing and storage

- Iron on **medium** heat while the fabric is still slightly damp; fully dried cotton needs steam or a spray bottle.
- Iron on the **reverse side** for printed pieces to protect the print surface.
- Store **clean and completely dry**, folded loosely, in a cotton bag rather than plastic. Plastic traps humidity and yellows cotton over a season.
- Add a **neem leaf sachet or cedar block** rather than naphthalene balls, which leave an odour in the weave.
- Refold along different lines each season so permanent creases do not set.

## Stain treatment

Treat every stain **before** the piece goes into a normal wash. Heat sets most stains permanently.

| Stain | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Tea, coffee | Rinse from the back with cold water, then a paste of mild detergent, 15 minutes |
| Turmeric, curry | Cold rinse, then sunlight on the wet spot only - turmeric photo-degrades |
| Oil, ghee | Cornflour or talc to absorb, brush off, then dish soap directly on the mark |
| Blood | Cold water only, immediately. Warm water sets it |
| Ink | Dab with isopropyl alcohol from behind, blot onto a cloth underneath |
| Sweat, body oils | Soak in cold water with a spoon of baking soda before washing |

Never rub a stain on a woven bedspread. Rubbing distorts the weave and creates a permanently shiny patch. Blot and flush instead.

## Quilted and filled bedspreads

If your piece has batting or is quilted through:

- Wash on **delicate**, alone, and only in a large-capacity machine.
- Support the weight when moving it wet - lifting a soaked quilt by one corner tears the internal stitching.
- Dry **flat** where possible, turning every few hours so the filling does not settle into one end.
- Expect 24-36 hours to dry fully. It must be dry all the way through the batting before storage.

## Quick answers

- **How often should a bedspread be washed?** Every 2-3 weeks with regular use; every 4-6 weeks if a top sheet sits underneath it.
- **Can I wash a cotton bedspread in a washing machine?** Yes, on a gentle cold cycle, provided the drum is large enough for it to move freely.
- **Will my bedspread shrink?** Some, on the first wash only. Cold water and low-heat drying keep it to a minimum.
- **Why did my bedspread fade?** Almost always direct sunlight while drying, or a detergent containing optical brighteners.
